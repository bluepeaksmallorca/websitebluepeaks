Massanella is the real thing: a mountain day, on a mountain that behaves like one. Weather comes in fast, the ground near the top is genuinely awkward, and the summit feels earned rather than delivered.

It is also the highest point on Mallorca you can legally stand on. **Puig Major** is 81 m higher, but its summit carries a military radar station and has been closed to the public for decades. Massanella is the walkers' summit.

## The route

The standard ascent starts at the **Coll de sa Bataia**, on the Ma-10 near the Lluc monastery, at about 600 m.

The lower section climbs steadily through holm oak forest on a good path. Around halfway it reaches the **Font des Prat**, a spring in a wooded hollow that is one of the more beautiful spots on the mountain and the natural place to stop. Near it are several **cases de neu** — the snow pits described in the FAQ below — which are worth the detour.

Above the spring the character changes. Trees give way to open mountain, and then to **karst limestone**: fissured, sharp-edged pavement that has to be walked across rather than along. Progress slows considerably here even though the gradient eases, and this is where the route feels long.

The summit is a broad stony dome with a trig point. On a clear day the view runs from the Bay of Palma to the northern cape, with the radomes of Puig Major dominating the near view.

Descent is by the same route.

## Access

This needs saying plainly: **access to Massanella has not been consistent**. Parts of the standard route cross private land, gates have opened and closed, and at times there has been a small charge at one of them. Reports online contradict each other because they were true at different moments.

We check the current position before each ascent. If you are going independently, ask locally on the day rather than trusting a blog post.

## Conditions

Massanella makes its own weather. Cloud can cover the summit within twenty minutes, and the karst is a bad place to be navigating by feel — the fissures are deep enough to break an ankle in.

In winter, snow and ice sit on the upper mountain for days at a time. It becomes a route that needs proper equipment rather than a walk.

## What we add

Honestly: on this route it is judgement and navigation, more than interpretation. Knowing when to turn back, knowing the line across the karst, knowing the current access arrangements. It is the route where having someone who walks it regularly makes the most concrete difference — and the one where we are most likely to tell a group it is not the right day for it.
