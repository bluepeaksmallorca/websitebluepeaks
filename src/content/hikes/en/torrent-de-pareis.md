The Torrent de Pareis is not a walk. It is a descent through a limestone canyon that in places is two hundred metres deep and only a few metres wide, and it is done by climbing over, under and between boulders the size of small buildings for most of a day.

It is the most serious route on Mallorca, and the one we are most likely to talk someone out of.

## What it actually involves

From **Escorca**, on the Ma-10, a path drops steeply into the riverbed. Once you are in the torrent, that is where you stay until the sea.

There is no path. There is a **line through the boulders**, and finding it is most of the skill. You will be using your hands constantly. There are perhaps a dozen sections where you have to lower yourself down a rock step with no obvious foothold, and several stretches of limestone polished glass-smooth by a few million years of floodwater.

Roughly halfway is the **Entreforc**, where the Torrent des Gorg Blau joins from the east under vertical walls. It is one of the most striking places on the island.

The final section narrows into the **Sa Calobra gorge** proper, opens onto a shingle beach between two cliffs, and ends in the sea. The swim at the end is not optional in any meaningful sense.

## The weather rule

This is the part that matters more than fitness.

The torrent drains a large catchment in the high Tramuntana. Heavy rain **anywhere in that catchment** — which may be several valleys away, invisible from where you stand, and hours earlier — can send water down the canyon fast. There is no escape route from most of the route. You cannot climb out.

So the rule is absolute: **no rain in the catchment, and none forecast.** Not "it looks fine here". This is why the route is a summer route, and why we will cancel it on a forecast that would be perfectly acceptable for any other walk on the island.

## Who it suits

People who are already comfortable scrambling, who have a head for heights, who are fit enough for eight hours of physical movement, and who actively want this rather than having read that it is famous.

It does not suit anyone who is unsure about any of the above. There is no shortening it and no bailing out halfway.

## Logistics

It is a linear route ending at Sa Calobra, which is connected to the rest of the island by a famously serpentine road and, in season, by **boat to Port de Sóller**. The boat is the pleasant way to finish. We arrange the return as part of the day.

## What we add

On this route the guiding is the point, not a bonus. Route-finding through the boulder field, judging the down-climbs, reading the forecast for a catchment you cannot see, carrying a rope and knowing when to use it, and having the standing to say the day is off. We take small groups on this one and we screen honestly beforehand — if we do not think it is right for your group, we will say so and suggest something else.
