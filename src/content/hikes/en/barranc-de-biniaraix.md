The Barranc de Biniaraix is the finest piece of dry-stone engineering on Mallorca, and one of the hardest three hours of walking on the island. It is a ravine above the hamlet of Biniaraix, just east of Sóller, and it has been paved with hand-laid steps for centuries — built so that people, mules and olives could move between the valley floor and the high pastures.

Walking it is the clearest way we know to understand what the **Serra de Tramuntana's UNESCO listing is actually for**. The World Heritage designation is not for the scenery. It is for the cultural landscape: the terraces, the water channels, the mule tracks, the charcoal-burning platforms — a system built by hand over roughly a thousand years to make a limestone range agriculturally viable.

## The route

The walk starts in the square at **Biniaraix**, a village of perhaps a hundred people. The first twenty minutes are gentle, past garden walls and irrigation channels still carrying water, with the ravine narrowing ahead.

Then it starts, and it does not really stop. The path becomes a **staircase of dry-stone steps**, switchbacking up the western wall of the gorge. Terraced olive groves rise on both sides, many of them still worked. You pass stone huts, cisterns, and a small waterfall that runs properly only after rain.

The gradient eases near the top, where the gorge opens onto pasture at the **Coll de l'Ofre**. From here there are choices. Our usual line turns west to **Es Cornadors**, a rock shoulder with a refuge on it and one of the best views on Mallorca — straight down into the Sóller valley with the sea beyond — and then descends by a different path back to Biniaraix, making a loop of about 12.5 km.

## The alternatives from the top

- **Puig de l'Ofre** (1,093 m) is a straightforward extra hour from the col, and an easy summit for the effort already spent.
- **Cúber reservoir** continues north-east on the GR221 and makes a linear day, which needs a car at each end or a taxi.
- **Straight back down** the gorge is the shortest version at about 8 km, and still a serious walk.

## Getting there and parking

Biniaraix has almost no parking and the streets are extremely narrow. Park in **Sóller** and walk the 25 minutes to Biniaraix, or take the local bus. Driving into the village and trying to turn around is a well-known way to lose an hour.

## When to go

Spring and autumn. March to May the gorge is green and the water is running; October and November are dry, clear and quiet. Avoid high summer entirely — the walls trap heat and there is nowhere to refill water.

## What we add

We guide this as a full day, and we pace it deliberately: the mistake almost everyone makes is climbing the first third too fast. Along the way we can point out what the different structures were for — which channels are still live, how the terraces are drained, why the steps are the height they are. It changes the walk from a stair climb into a reading of a landscape.
