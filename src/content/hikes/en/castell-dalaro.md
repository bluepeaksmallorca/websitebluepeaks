Castell d'Alaró sits on a limestone bluff at 822 metres, above the village of Alaró on the southern edge of the Serra de Tramuntana. The first written record of the castle dates from 902, and it held out against a siege for two years after the Catalan conquest of 1229 — which is roughly how long it takes to appreciate the position once you are standing on it. On a clear day you can see the whole central plain of the island, the bay of Palma, and the ridgeline running north towards Puig Major.

It is the walk we suggest most often to people on their first day in the mountains. The path is unambiguous, the surface is good, and the reward arrives at a sensible ratio to the effort.

## The route

Almost everyone starts from **Es Verger**, a farmhouse restaurant roughly 4 km up a narrow road from Alaró village. From there a broad stone track climbs steadily through holm oak and carob, turning north as it gains height.

The character changes about two-thirds of the way up. The track narrows into a **cobbled staircase**, laid centuries ago for the pilgrims and garrison who used this route, and passes through a gate cut into the cliff itself. This is the steepest section and the one people remember. It is well built and needs no scrambling, but it is genuinely uphill.

Above the gate the ground opens onto a broad summit plateau. The castle ruins occupy the northern edge; the **Santuari de la Mare de Déu del Refugi**, a small sanctuary with a simple restaurant and guest rooms, sits just below them.

The return is by the same route. Loose stones on the cobbles make the descent slower than people expect, and it is where most turned ankles happen.

## Starting from the village instead

If you would rather not drive the road to Es Verger, the walk can start in **Alaró village**. That adds roughly 6 km and 350 m of ascent for the round trip and turns it into a full day of five to six hours. It is a better walk — the lower section climbs through terraced olive groves that most visitors never see — and it removes the parking problem entirely.

## When to go

October to May is the right window. The route is south-facing and largely unshaded, and in July and August the climb is genuinely unpleasant after about ten in the morning. In winter it is close to perfect: cool, clear, and quiet.

Weekends are busy year-round, and the parking at Es Verger fills by mid-morning. A weekday start before nine avoids almost all of it.
