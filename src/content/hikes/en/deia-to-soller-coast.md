This is the route we suggest for a first day, for mixed groups, and for anyone who wants the Tramuntana without the suffering. It runs along the north-west coast between two of the best-known villages on the island, mostly through olive terraces and pine woods, and it finishes at a harbour where you can swim and get a boat.

## The route

From **Deià** — a village of stone houses stacked on a hillside, long colonised by writers and musicians, and still beautiful despite it — the path drops towards the sea.

**Cala Deià** comes up after about forty minutes: a shingle cove between cliffs with clear, cold water and a couple of restaurants built onto the rocks. It is the natural first stop, and in warm months the swim there is half the reason to do the walk.

From the cove the path climbs back onto the terraces and turns north-east, contouring above the sea through olive groves that have been worked for centuries. There are a few rocky steps and one steeper pull, but the walking is easy and the view is continuous.

The route passes below the **Muleta lighthouse** and then descends into **Port de Sóller**, a horseshoe bay with a promenade, restaurants, and the terminus of the vintage tram that runs up to Sóller town.

## Getting there and back

It is linear. The usual arrangement is bus from Sóller to Deià in the morning and the tram or a taxi at the end, but there are several combinations depending on where you are staying. We sort this out with you in advance — it is the one part of this walk that people get wrong on their own.

## When to go

Almost any time. The route is north-facing, largely shaded, and cooled by sea breeze, which makes it one of the few classic Tramuntana walks that works in summer.

Winter is excellent: clear light, empty paths, and the restaurants in Deià still open. Note that the Cala Deià restaurants are seasonal and shut in winter.

## What we add

The walking is easy enough that this is a day about what you notice. The terraces, and how much labour is standing in them. Which of the olive trees are five hundred years old. The village and who has lived in it. Where the water comes from. And the practical part: getting the timing right so you arrive at the cove when the light is on the water and before the crowd.
