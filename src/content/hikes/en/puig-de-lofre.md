If someone has one day in the mountains and wants to stand on a summit, this is the one we suggest. Puig de l'Ofre gives a genuine mountain top — a proper cairn, a 360-degree view, a sense of having arrived somewhere — for a level of effort that most reasonably active people can manage.

## The route

The walk begins at the **Cúber reservoir**, at about 750 m, which means a third of the height is already done when you park. The Ma-10 mountain road delivers you straight there.

From the car park a broad track runs along the northern shore of the reservoir, flat and easy, giving twenty minutes to warm up while looking at the day ahead. The distinctive cone of l'Ofre sits directly in front, and it looks steeper from here than it is.

At the western end of the reservoir the track climbs gently through pasture towards the **Coll de l'Ofre**, a broad saddle at around 870 m. This is a crossroads: the Barranc de Biniaraix drops away south-west towards Sóller, and the GR221 continues north.

The summit path leaves the col and turns north-east, steepening onto stony ground. The final section is a short, easy **scramble on solid limestone** — hands out of pockets, but nothing exposed and nothing that needs skill.

## The view

Worth being specific about, because it is the reason to come. From the cairn you can see **Puig Major** (1,445 m, the island's highest, topped with military radomes) directly north, the whole **Sóller valley** dropping away to the west with the sea beyond, the **Cúber and Gorg Blau reservoirs** below, and on a clear day the plain running out to the eastern coast.

It is also one of the more reliable places on the island to see **black vultures**. The Mallorcan population came back from around twenty birds in the 1980s to a few hundred now, and they use this ridge.

## Combining it with the Barranc

The strongest day we guide on this side of the range links the two: up the **Barranc de Biniaraix** from the village, over the Coll de l'Ofre, up to the summit, and down to Cúber. About 15 km and 1,100 m of ascent, and it takes in the best staircase and the best viewpoint in the Tramuntana in one go.

It is linear, so it needs a car at Cúber or a taxi from Sóller. We arrange that as part of the day.

## When to go

Spring and autumn are ideal. The approach track is exposed and the summit is windy in almost all conditions — bring a layer even in June, because the temperature drop at the top is real.

Winter ascents are good but check for ice: the north-facing final section holds it after a cold snap.
