Most walking paths in the Serra de Tramuntana were built for a reason — moving olives, charcoal, snow, or people who had to be somewhere. The Camí de s'Arxiduc is one of the few built purely so that someone could look at the view.

That someone was **Archduke Ludwig Salvator of Austria**, who arrived on Mallorca in 1867, fell for the north-west coast, bought a long stretch of it, and spent decades having paths, miradors and stone benches built across his estates. He was an unusual landlord: he protected the woodland, forbade tree felling, and wrote a monumental survey of the Balearic islands. The path above Valldemossa is the best-known thing he left behind.

## The route

The loop starts in **Valldemossa** and climbs steeply out of the top of the village — the hardest section is in the first hour, which is a reasonable trade.

Above the treeline the path reaches an open limestone plateau, and then turns north-west onto the **balcony section**. This is what people come for: a built path running along the cliff top, roughly 400 metres above the sea, with the coast falling away to Deià and Sóller and nothing between you and the water.

Beyond the balcony the route turns inland across open ground, with the option to add **Es Teix** (1,064 m) — an extra hour, a summit, and a wider view including the interior of the island.

The descent returns to Valldemossa through woodland, past the *Font de s'Abeurada*, on a gentler line than the ascent.

## The exposure question

Worth being direct about. The cliff-top section is wide enough to walk comfortably and has no scrambling, but there is **no barrier and the drop is serious**. In strong tramuntana wind we do not take groups along it, and if anyone is uncomfortable with heights we use the inland variant, which loses the balcony but keeps the summit and most of the view.

Tell us in advance if exposure is a concern. It changes which version of this walk we do, not whether we do it.

## Valldemossa

The village is worth an hour at either end. **Chopin and George Sand** spent the winter of 1838–39 in the former Carthusian monastery here — miserably, by her account, in a cold cell with a bad piano — and the resulting book made the place famous. It is now one of the most visited villages on the island, which is a reason to start walking early and come back down for a late lunch.

## When to go

Spring and autumn. The plateau is exposed and hot in summer, and the cliff path in July is unpleasant by ten in the morning.

For photography, plan around **sunset**: the coast faces north-west, and the last hour of light along the balcony is the best on the island. That commits you to descending in the dark, which is straightforward with head torches and someone who knows the way.

## What we add

Route choice, mainly — which variant fits your group, and whether the wind makes the balcony sensible today. Plus the history, which genuinely changes this walk: knowing why a Habsburg archduke built a footpath along a cliff makes the stonework under your feet considerably more interesting.
