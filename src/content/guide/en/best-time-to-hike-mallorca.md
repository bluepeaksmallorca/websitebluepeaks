Mallorca has a longer hiking season than most of Europe and a shorter one than its climate suggests. The island is walkable for roughly nine months of the year, but the two months at the centre of the tourist season are the two worst months to be climbing anything.

Here is how the year actually breaks down in the Serra de Tramuntana.

## The short answer

**March to early June, and September to November.** Those are the months when the temperature, the daylight and the ground conditions all line up. If you can only travel in a fixed window, aim for **April** or **October**.

## Spring: March to early June

The strongest season. The range comes out of winter green, the water is still running in the gorges and torrents, and the wildflowers on the terraces are worth the trip on their own. Almond blossom is late January to mid-February — earlier than most people expect, and not really hiking season, though it is beautiful.

- **March** — cool, 15–19 °C, some rain, very quiet. Excellent for long ascents.
- **April** — the best all-round month. 17–22 °C, everything green, long daylight.
- **May** — warm, 20–25 °C, reliably dry. High routes are at their best.
- **Early June** — still good, but the heat starts to matter by midday.

## Summer: mid-June to August

We do not guide long ascents in this window, and we would gently steer you away from planning one.

Inland temperatures regularly pass 33 °C, the limestone holds heat, and shade in the Tramuntana is scarce. The gorges are worse than the ridges, not better — they trap still air. Dehydration, not difficulty, is what causes problems on Mallorcan trails in August.

What does work in summer:

- **Pre-dawn starts**, finishing by ten or eleven
- **Coastal routes** with sea breeze and swimming at the end
- **North-facing paths** in the Sóller and Deià area
- **Short routes** with a clear escape option

## Autumn: September to November

The second strong season, and our own favourite.

- **September** — still hot in the first half, excellent in the second. Sea is warm.
- **October** — near-perfect walking, 18–23 °C. The busiest of the good months.
- **November** — cool, clear, quiet, and the light is at its best. Some rain.

Autumn is also the wettest part of the year in the Tramuntana. This usually means a heavy downpour for an hour rather than a grey week, but it does mean torrents can run fast, and any route involving a gorge bed needs checking on the day.

## Winter: December to February

Underrated for walking, with real limits.

The island is quiet, the air is clear, and mid-altitude routes are genuinely pleasant at 12–16 °C. Many restaurants and some mountain refuges close, buses run reduced timetables, and daylight drops to about nine and a half hours — enough for most day routes, but not for long ones with a late start.

Above roughly 1,000 m, snow and ice are possible from January. Puig Major and Massanella can hold ice on north-facing ground for days. This does not make winter unsuitable; it makes checking conditions non-negotiable.

## A note on rain and the torrents

Routes that follow a torrent bed — the Torrent de Pareis above all — are the one case where weather matters far more than it appears. A dry riverbed can become impassable within hours of heavy rain in the catchment above, which may be miles away and out of sight. If a route follows a watercourse, the forecast for the whole catchment is what counts, not the forecast where you are standing.

## Planning around all of it

If your dates are fixed and they fall in high summer, the honest answer is that you can still have an excellent walking day here — it just has to start in the dark and finish before lunch. Tell us your dates and we will tell you plainly what is realistic in them.
