Mallorca has better public transport than most Mediterranean islands, and for hiking it is often the better option — not just the cheaper one. Linear routes are the best walking here, and linear routes and hire cars do not get along.

## The network

**TIB buses** (Transports de les Illes Balears) run the island. The relevant ones for walkers are the 200-series along the Ma-10 mountain road and the routes serving the north-west villages. Fares are low, buses are modern, and timetables thin out considerably outside the main season.

**The Sóller train** is a narrow-gauge wooden railway from Palma to Sóller, opened in 1912, that climbs through the mountains and a long tunnel. It is slow and priced as an attraction rather than as transport — but worth doing once.

**The Sóller tram** links Sóller town with Port de Sóller, and is the standard way to finish the Deià coastal walk.

**Boats** run in season between Port de Sóller, Sa Calobra and Cala Tuent. The Sa Calobra boat is the civilised way to end the Torrent de Pareis.

## Trailhead by trailhead

**Sóller and Biniaraix** — Well served. Train from Palma, buses from Palma and along the coast. Biniaraix is a 25-minute walk from Sóller. *Do not drive into Biniaraix.*

**Cúber reservoir** (for Puig de l'Ofre) — The 354 between Sóller and Lluc stops here on request, a handful of times a day. Check the current timetable; it changes seasonally.

**Lluc and Coll de sa Bataia** (for Massanella) — Buses from Inca and along the Ma-10.

**Valldemossa** (for the Camí de s'Arxiduc) — Frequent buses from Palma. Parking exists but fills early.

**Deià** — Buses along the coast road between Valldemossa and Sóller.

**Alaró** — Buses from Palma and Inca. But the walk from the village up to Es Verger is 4 km on a narrow road, which is why most people drive it. If you are taking the bus, plan the long version of the route from the village — it is the better walk anyway.

**Sa Calobra** — Boat from Port de Sóller in season, or the 355 bus. Both seasonal.

## Where parking is the real problem

For several routes, having a car is the harder option:

- **Es Verger** (Castell d'Alaró) — a steep single-track road with limited space at the top
- **Cúber** — roadside lay-bys that fill by nine on spring weekends
- **Biniaraix** — streets too narrow to turn around in
- **Valldemossa** — paid parking that fills early

## Planning around timetables

The honest constraint is frequency. On the mountain routes you may have three or four buses a day, and out of season fewer. That means the timetable determines the walk, not the reverse — and it means checking the *current* schedule rather than one you saw online, because TIB changes them seasonally.

Check timetables at **tib.org**.

## What we do about it

When we plan a day with you, transport is part of it. That might mean picking you up, meeting you at a bus stop, arranging a taxi at one end of a linear route, or choosing a different route entirely because the return connection does not work on the day you want. It is unglamorous and it is the part visitors most often get wrong on their own.
