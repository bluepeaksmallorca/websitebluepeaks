People ask which month is best, and we give the sensible answer — April, October — but it is not really how the year feels when you are out in it every week.

## February: the blossom, and nobody there

The almond trees come out in the last week of January and hold for about three weeks. The plain below Alaró turns white and pink, and for a fortnight the island looks like a postcard nobody has printed yet, because almost no visitors are here to see it.

The walking in February is cold, clear and empty. We do lower routes — the coast, the foothills, the terraces — and the light is the hardest, cleanest light of the year. If you can stand the short days, it is our favourite secret.

## April: everything at once

By April the range is green in a way it will not be again until next spring. The torrents still have water in them. The Barranc de Biniaraix runs, properly, with a waterfall that exists for about six weeks a year.

This is when the terraces make sense. The orchid species come up in the meadows above Alaró. Everything is doing something.

It is also when we work most, and when dates go first.

## June: the turn

There is a specific week, usually in the middle of June, when it stops being spring. You feel it on a climb — the same ascent that was pleasant a fortnight ago is now work by ten in the morning.

After that week we start moving the days earlier and earlier.

## August: five in the morning

August guiding means meeting people in the dark. We are walking by half past five, on the summit by eight, and down before the heat arrives properly. It sounds punishing and it is the opposite — the range at first light in August, with nobody on it, is one of the best things we do all year.

The afternoons belong to nobody. Everything stops. It is too hot to be out and the island goes quiet in a way that has nothing to do with tourism.

## November: the light

October gets the reputation and November gets the light.

By November the heat has gone, the first rain has settled the dust, and the sun sits low enough all day that the ridgelines stack in layers all the way to the horizon. The vultures are easier to see against a lower sky. The restaurants are still open and the paths are almost empty.

If someone asks us privately when to come, and they do not need to travel in school holidays, we say the first three weeks of November.

## And then it starts again

By late January someone will send a photo of the first almond tree, and the year turns over.

The routes on this site do not change much between visits. What is on them changes constantly, and that is most of the reason we are still doing this.
